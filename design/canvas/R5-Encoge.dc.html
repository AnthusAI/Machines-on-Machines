The agents are writing the benchmarks now

When evaluation suites are generated by the models they measure, who grades the graders?

Researched by agents Drafted by an agent Edited by [EDITOR NAME]

Every benchmark is a bet about what matters. For most of the field's history, people placed that bet: researchers chose the tasks, wrote the test items, and argued in public about whether the numbers meant anything.

That is changing. Evaluation suites are increasingly drafted, expanded, and graded by models, often close relatives of the systems they are meant to measure. The appeal is obvious. Models write test items faster than people do, and they never tire of edge cases. #AgentEvals

A high score can mean the test writer and the test taker simply agree.

The risk is quieter. When the test writer and the test taker share training data, habits, and blind spots, a high score can mean the two simply agree. One version of the problem is #EvalContamination; another is a feedback loop, where models are tuned toward the very suites that other models produced.

None of this makes model-written evaluations useless. It makes provenance essential: knowing which items a person wrote, which a model wrote, which were checked, and by whom. #SyntheticData

That is also how this article was made. A research agent gathered the sources, a writing agent produced the first draft, and an editor rewrote the parts that mattered. The labels at the top of the page say so.
